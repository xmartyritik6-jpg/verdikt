About Verdikt

Verdikt exists because most product review sites make buying decisions harder, not easier. Star ratings get inflated, sponsored listings blend into "top picks," and real trade-offs get buried under marketing language. We built Verdikt to fix that for Indian shoppers comparing earbuds, smartphones, laptops, and smartwatches.

What we actually do

Every product on Verdikt gets a Trust Score — a rating built from three things: how closely the product matches its advertised specs, what owners consistently report about reliability, and value for the price paid. It is not based on review volume or star counts, which are easy to inflate.

What we don't do: we are not a lab. We don't run controlled durability tests or measure battery life with instruments. Our scores come from published specifications, manufacturer claims, and patterns in what owners report — read carefully and weighed against price. Where a claim is ours rather than measured, we say so. If that changes and we start testing units ourselves, we'll say that too.

We also write out honest pros and cons for every product — including the trade-offs a brand's own marketing won't mention — and track price history so you can tell a genuine discount from an inflated "was" price.

How we make money

Verdikt uses affiliate links — if you buy a product through a link on this site, we may earn a small commission from the retailer (such as Amazon or Flipkart) at no extra cost to you. This never affects a product's Trust Score or its place in our rankings. Any sponsored placement, if introduced in future, will always be clearly labeled as such.

A work in progress

Verdikt is a new, independently built platform — not affiliated with Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus, Vivo, boAt, Noise, Fire-Boltt, or any other brand featured here. How Verdikt works
Do you actually test these products yourselves?

No, and we’d rather say so plainly. Verdikt is not a lab. We don’t buy units, run battery rundowns, measure screen brightness with instruments, or drop-test anything.

What we do is read the published specifications carefully, compare them against what the manufacturer claims in its marketing, and weigh the result against the price. Where owners consistently report the same problem, that shapes the “where it falls short” list.

That’s a real service — but it is not the same as hands-on testing, and you should treat it accordingly. If we ever start testing units ourselves, we will say so on the page.

What is the Trust Score, and how is it calculated?

It’s our own judgement, expressed as a number out of 100, built from three things: how closely a product matches its advertised specs, what owners consistently report about reliability, and what you get for the price compared with alternatives in the same bracket.

It is deliberately not based on star ratings or review counts, because those are the easiest numbers on the internet to inflate.

We don’t publish fixed percentage weightings, because we don’t use them — quoting “40% this, 30% that” would suggest a precision that isn’t there. Treat the score as an informed opinion, and read the pros and cons underneath it, which is where the actual reasoning lives.

How do you decide which products to cover?

Right now we cover smartphones sold in India, chosen by what people are actually shopping for across price bands — roughly ₹13,000 to ₹85,000.

No brand has paid to be listed, and no brand has been left out for refusing to. Other categories — earbuds, laptops, smartwatches — will come back once we’ve verified their data the same way we’re doing for phones.

Prices and links
How often are prices updated?

Manually, and not on a fixed schedule — typically once or twice a week, more often around festive sales.

This is an honest limitation. We don’t scrape retailers automatically, so the price you see is what we recorded when we last checked. Always confirm on the retailer’s page before buying. Every product page tells you this too.

Why does the price history say “not tracked long enough yet”?

Because we genuinely haven’t tracked it long enough, and we’d rather tell you that than draw a chart out of thin air.

A price history chart is only useful if the points on it are real observations. Ours started recently. Once there are enough recorded price changes to say something meaningful — whether today’s price is a good one or an inflated one — the chart will appear on its own.

In the meantime, setting a price alert is the more useful move.

Why do some products say “Link coming soon”?

Because we haven’t yet confirmed a retailer listing for the exact variant shown on that page.

Phone models are sold in several storage and RAM configurations, at prices that can differ by ₹10,000 or more. Linking to the wrong one sends you to a product that isn’t what you just read about. Until we’ve checked which listing matches, we leave the button off rather than guess.

The price here doesn’t match what I see on Amazon or Flipkart. Why?

Most likely one of two things: the price moved since we last checked, or you’re looking at a different storage variant.

Retailer prices change daily, and the same phone can appear at very different prices for 128GB and 256GB. The retailer’s page is always the authority — if ours is wrong, tell us and we’ll correct it.

Pick a product, enter your email and the price you’d be happy to pay. When we record that product at or below your price, you get one email. That’s the whole thing.
